## A typical workday, hour by hour

**Here is how a normal day runs for a designer on our team.** Times vary with deadlines, but the rhythm stays much the same.

### 9:00 am: Morning check-in and briefs

The day starts with a short team meeting. Account managers share new client requests, urgent changes and the day's priorities. A designer might receive five or six briefs, such as:

- Three social media posts for a beauty clinic in Nugegoda
- A Facebook lead-generation ad for a property developer
- A promotion carousel for a travel agency
- A logo revision for a new startup

Each brief explains the goal, the audience, the message and the deadline. Good designers ask questions now rather than guess later.

### 9:30 am: Planning and research

Before opening Photoshop, designers look at the client's brand guidelines, past posts and competitors. For a new campaign, they gather references and sketch two or three rough ideas. Ten minutes of thinking here saves an hour of redesigning later.

### 10:00 am to 1:00 pm: Deep design work

This is the most focused part of the day. Designers create:

- **Social media creatives:** posts, carousels and stories in Photoshop, Illustrator or Canva.
- **Ad creatives:** several versions of the same ad so the marketing team can test which works best.
- **Local-language designs:** many campaigns need Sinhala and Tamil versions, so clean local typography is a real skill.

[PHOTO: a designer working on a campaign]

### 1:00 pm: Lunch

A proper break matters. Creative work is tiring, and fresh eyes after lunch catch mistakes you missed in the morning.

### 2:00 pm: Feedback and revisions

Account managers share client feedback on yesterday's designs. One client wants a bigger logo, another wants a different headline. Handling feedback calmly and quickly is one of the most important skills in agency life. Every revision teaches you something about what clients and audiences respond to.

### 3:00 pm: Reels, motion and shoots

Short video is now a big part of the job. Designers turn static posts into simple animations, add text and branding to reels, and edit content in After Effects, Premiere Pro or CapCut. Some days include a location shoot, where designers and video creators film content for a restaurant, salon or brand.

[PHOTO: behind the scenes at a reel shoot]

### 4:30 pm: Quality check and handover

Before anything goes to a client, designs are checked for spelling, brand colours, logo placement and readability on a phone screen. Final files are exported in the right sizes for Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and Google Ads, then handed to the account team.

### 5:30 pm: Wrap-up and learning

Designers update their task list for tomorrow. Many spend a few minutes looking at new design trends, AI tools or campaigns from other brands, because staying current is part of the job.

## Skills that help you succeed in this role

**Talent gets you hired, but these habits help you grow fast in an agency:**

- **Speed with quality.** Agencies handle many briefs each day, so working efficiently matters.
- **Attention to detail.** One wrong phone number or misspelt word can cost a client real business.
- **Openness to feedback.** The best designers see revisions as part of the process, not a criticism.
- **Teamwork.** You work closely with account managers, writers, video editors and ad specialists.
- **Curiosity.** New platforms, formats and AI tools appear every year.

## Is agency graphic design right for you?

**Agency design suits people who enjoy variety, deadlines and seeing their work go live quickly.** It may be a great fit if:

- You get bored designing for one brand all year
- You like a fast-paced, team-based environment
- You want to build a strong, varied portfolio early in your career
- You are interested in marketing, not just visuals

If you prefer slower, long-term projects for a single brand, an in-house role may suit you better. Many designers start at an agency to learn quickly, then decide on their long-term path.
