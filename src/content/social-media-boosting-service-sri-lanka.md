## What is boosting, and why does it often waste money?

A "boost" is a simple ad made from a post that's already on your Facebook page or Instagram account. You choose a budget and a few days, and Facebook shows the post to more people.

The problem is what happens by default:

- **The wrong goal.** Boosts often default to "engagement", which means Facebook finds people who like and comment, not people who message or buy. You get likes, not customers.
- **Too broad an audience.** "People in Sri Lanka aged 18–65" includes millions of people who can't or won't buy from you. A Kandy bakery doesn't need customers in Jaffna.
- **The wrong post.** Not every post deserves money. Boosting a festival greeting rarely sells anything. Boosting a clear offer with a price usually does.
- **No tracking.** Without a WhatsApp button, a lead form or a pixel on your website, you can't tell which boost brought customers.
- **Stop and start.** Random boosts every few weeks never give Facebook enough data to learn who your buyers are.

None of this means boosting is bad. It means boosting works when someone sets it up properly. That's what we do.

## What makes a post worth boosting?

Before you spend a rupee, a post should pass this checklist:

1. **It has a clear offer or reason to act.** A price, a deal, a deadline, a new product.
2. **It shows the product or result clearly** in the first second. On mobile, people scroll fast.
3. **It's short.** One idea per post. For video, the first three seconds matter most.
4. **It tells people what to do next.** "WhatsApp us to book", "Call now", "Order online".
5. **It's in the language your buyers use.** Sinhala, Tamil, English or a mix. Many Sri Lankan brands get better results with Sinhala or Tamil captions for local audiences.
6. **It has a working contact route.** A WhatsApp button, a phone number or a link that loads quickly on mobile.

If your posts don't pass this checklist, boosting won't fix that. Our [social media design](/social-media-design-sri-lanka) and [reel creation](/reel-creation-sri-lanka) services can make posts that are built to be boosted.

## How we judge whether a boost is working

Likes and reach look nice, but they don't pay the bills. These are the numbers we watch, and the ones you'll see in your report:

- **Cost per message.** How much you paid for each WhatsApp or Messenger conversation. For most businesses this is the most important number.
- **Cost per lead or booking.** If people fill in a form or book online, how much each one cost.
- **Click-through rate.** The share of people who saw the post and clicked. A low rate usually means the post or offer isn't clear enough.
- **Frequency.** How many times the same person saw your post. When it climbs too high, people get tired of it, and we refresh the creative.
- **Sales you tell us about.** We ask you how many messages turned into customers. That's how we know which boosts are really worth the money.

If a boost isn't bringing messages at a sensible cost after the learning period, we stop it and move the budget elsewhere. You'll never pay to keep a boost running just because it's getting likes.

## Common boosting mistakes we fix

**Boosting from your personal profile's phone app at midnight.** Quick boosts from the app often use default settings. We set every campaign up deliberately.

**Sending people to your page instead of WhatsApp.** If you want messages, the button should open a chat with you, not your page's timeline.

**Using the same post for months.** Even a great post wears out. We rotate fresh posts and reels so your audience doesn't tune out.

**Targeting "all of Sri Lanka" for a local shop.** A tighter area usually brings cheaper, more useful messages.

**Not answering the messages.** Boosts bring conversations, and slow replies lose them. We'll suggest quick replies and saved answers so you can respond fast, even when you're busy.
