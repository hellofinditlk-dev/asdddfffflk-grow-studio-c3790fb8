## How Newspaper Ad Rates Are Calculated

Most Sri Lankan newspapers price display ads by **column centimetre (col cm)**: the width of the ad in columns multiplied by its height in centimetres. Full-page, half-page and quarter-page ads are usually priced as fixed packages.

The final price depends on:

- **The newspaper:** national dailies and Sunday papers with larger readership cost more.
- **Language edition:** Sinhala, Tamil and English papers reach different audiences and have different rate cards.
- **Day of the week:** Sunday editions usually cost more than weekday editions because readership is higher.
- **Page position:** front page, back page, page 3 and special pages carry a premium.
- **Colour or black and white:** colour ads cost more.
- **Size:** larger ads cost more, but the cost per col cm can fall for bigger bookings.
- **Frequency:** booking several insertions can reduce the price per ad.

## Sunday Papers vs Weekday Papers

Sunday editions such as the Sunday Times, Sunday Lankadeepa and Sunday Virakesari are read at home, often by the whole family, and kept for longer. That makes them popular for property, education and big-ticket retail ads, and explains why Sunday rates are higher.

Weekday papers work well for time-sensitive offers, recruitment and announcements that need to appear on a specific date.

## Tips to Get More From Your Newspaper Ad

- **One clear message.** Readers scan pages quickly.
- **Strong headline and image.** They decide whether people stop and read.
- **Include a phone number and WhatsApp.** Most enquiries come by phone or chat.
- **Add a QR code** linking to a landing page, so you can measure response.
- **Repeat.** Several smaller insertions often beat one large ad.
- **Match the language** to the paper's readers rather than translating word for word.
