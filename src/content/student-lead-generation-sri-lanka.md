# Student Lead Generation in Sri Lanka – How Schools and Campuses Fill Intakes

For private higher education institutes, international schools, tuition classes and training providers in Sri Lanka, every intake depends on one thing: a steady flow of student enquiries that turn into enrolments. Student lead generation is the process of attracting those enquiries online, capturing their details and following up until they register.

This guide explains how online student lead generation works in Sri Lanka, which channels perform best, and why follow-up matters as much as the ads themselves.

## What Is a Student Lead?

A student lead is a prospective student, or a parent, who has shown interest and shared contact details, for example by:

- Filling in an enquiry or application form
- Sending a WhatsApp or Messenger message
- Calling your admissions team
- Registering for an open day, webinar or seminar
- Downloading a course guide or fee structure

## The Student Decision Journey in Sri Lanka

Choosing a course is a big decision, often made together with parents. Most students:

1. **Discover** options on social media, YouTube or through friends.
2. **Research** on Google: course names, fees, entry requirements, university partners.
3. **Compare** several institutes, often by messaging each one.
4. **Visit or attend** an open day, seminar or consultation.
5. **Decide** before the intake deadline, often after a scholarship or payment-plan conversation.

Good lead generation reaches students at every one of these steps.

## Landing Pages That Convert Students

Sending ads to your homepage wastes budget. Each programme or intake should have a focused page with:

- Course name, duration, awarding body and intake date
- Entry requirements in simple language
- Fees or a clear "request fee structure" option
- Scholarships and payment plans
- Student testimonials and graduate outcomes
- A short form and a WhatsApp button

## Follow-Up: Where Most Student Leads Are Lost

Many institutes generate plenty of enquiries but enrol only a small share. The usual reasons are slow replies, no follow-up and no tracking.

- **Reply within minutes**, not hours. Students message several institutes at once.
- **Call and WhatsApp.** Many students respond on WhatsApp but decide after a call.
- **Follow up several times** before the intake deadline.
- **Track every lead** from enquiry to enrolment.

## Timing Your Campaigns Around Intakes

- **Start 8–12 weeks before** each intake to build awareness.
- **Increase budget** 3–4 weeks before the deadline.
- **Run open day and seminar campaigns** to create urgency.
- **Retarget** everyone who visited your course pages but didn't enquire.
- **Plan around A/L and O/L results** releases, when many students start looking. Use announcements from the [Department of Examinations](https://www.doenets.lk/) for each exam cycle rather than assuming a recurring release date. Launch dates should follow confirmed results and your actual intake deadline.

| Lead-to-visit rate | How many enquiries attend an open day or consultation |

### Which platform is best for student lead generation?
Google Search captures students already looking for a course, while Facebook, Instagram and TikTok create awareness and drive open-day registrations. Most institutes use both.

### Do lead forms or WhatsApp messages work better?
Lead forms give you contact details quickly, while WhatsApp starts a real conversation. Many institutes use both and follow up every lead fast.

### Can digital marketing work for tuition classes?
Yes. Tuition classes can use local targeting, Facebook and TikTok content and WhatsApp to fill classes in specific areas.
