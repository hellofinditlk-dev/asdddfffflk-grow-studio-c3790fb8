### Understanding the services before recommending them

You do not need to memorise technical terms to have a useful sales conversation. You need to understand the customer's problem, explain what a service covers and recognise when a question should go to the delivery team. A restaurant asking for more bookings may need a different conversation from a tuition class preparing for a new intake or a shop launching an online catalogue.

| Service family | Questions to ask a prospect | What to verify before quoting |
|---|---|---|
| Social media management | Which pages do you use, and who prepares your content now? | Platforms, content quantities, approvals and package scope |
| Facebook and Google advertising | What action should a customer take after seeing an ad? | Campaign objective, service fee, separate ad budget and tracking |
| SEO and AI visibility | Which services and locations should people discover? | Website condition, agreed work and reporting; never promise a ranking |
| Graphic design and branding | What needs designing, and where will it be used? | Deliverables, formats, revisions and usage requirements |
| Video and reels | Who is the audience, and what should the video communicate? | Creative brief, production scope, assets and approvals |
| Lead generation and follow-up | Who responds to enquiries, and what happens after the first call? | Qualification questions, handover process and responsibilities |

Use the current service pages and approved proposal when explaining deliverables. Do not assume that every package includes the same platforms, videos, revisions or advertising budget. If a prospect asks for something outside the stated scope, record the request and ask the team to confirm it rather than adding an informal promise to win the sale.

The same principle applies to creative examples. Show only work that the agency has approved for sharing. Explain whether an example illustrates a design style, a service deliverable or a documented result. A picture of a campaign does not, by itself, prove how many enquiries or sales it generated.

## How you earn: the commission model

Earnings are commission-based. Exact new-sale commission, recurring commission duration and rate, monthly targets, bonuses, upsell and referral rewards are confirmed in writing before you start. There is no confirmed base allowance or guaranteed monthly income stated here.

Commission is based on payments clients actually make. Your written offer must explain eligibility, cancellations and refunds, the payment date and method, and whether call costs are company-funded, reimbursed or paid by you. Do not assume a company SIM or a call allowance.

No registration fee, training fee, deposit or starter-kit fee is charged.

### Questions to settle before accepting a commission-based role

A commission arrangement should be understandable before you start selling. Ask what counts as your sale, when it becomes eligible for commission and how you can review the calculation. For a monthly client, clarify whether recurring commission applies to renewals, how long eligibility lasts and what happens if the client pauses or changes package. None of these details should be guessed from a sample pitch.

Also ask who approves discounts and how commission is affected by an approved discount, tax, advertising spend or a partially paid invoice. These are questions for the written offer, not a statement that the agency uses a particular calculation. Keep a copy of the agreed terms and raise any unclear item before accepting the role.

For your own budgeting, avoid treating potential commission as money already earned. An enquiry can become a proposal without becoming a paid client. Compare the agreed work pattern with your other commitments and personal expenses. If you need a fixed monthly income, ask directly whether any basic allowance is offered; this page does not confirm one.

## From enquiry to client: a practical sales workflow

The following workflow is preparation guidance for applicants, not a guaranteed conversion process or a numerical performance target. It shows the organisation and judgement involved in selling marketing services. Your actual scripts, reporting tools and approval process are explained by the team.

### Read the enquiry before making contact

Check the prospect's name, business, service of interest and any message they submitted. Notice whether they requested a price, a consultation or help with a particular issue. This prevents a generic pitch that ignores the reason they contacted the agency. If the information is incomplete, use the first conversation to clarify it rather than assuming the business's needs.

### Open politely and ask permission to continue

Introduce yourself and Cypher Digital, explain that you are following up on their enquiry and ask whether it is a convenient time. An illustrative opening is: “Hello, I am following up on your enquiry about marketing for your business. Is now a suitable time to ask a few questions?” This is a practice example, not an approved agency script. If the person is busy, agree a callback instead of pressing ahead.

### Understand the business goal

Ask what the business sells, which customers it wants to reach and what it hopes to improve. “More customers” may mean appointment bookings, course enquiries, website orders or better follow-up of existing leads. Ask who currently manages marketing and who makes the final decision. Listen carefully and summarise the problem back to the prospect so they can correct any misunderstanding.

### Recommend a relevant next step

Connect the proposed service to the stated need. Explain scope in ordinary language and distinguish agency fees from advertising spend or other separately agreed costs. If the answer depends on a website review, creative brief or campaign assessment, arrange that next step rather than pretending a single package fits every business. Never guarantee a number of sales, a Google position or an AI recommendation.

### Follow up with context, not pressure

Record what was discussed, what information is missing and when the prospect wants to hear from you again. A useful follow-up refers to the agreed question or proposal, rather than repeatedly asking whether they are ready to pay. If they decline or ask not to be contacted, respect that decision and update the record. Professional persistence is organised follow-up, not unwanted repeated calling.

### Close accurately and hand over clearly

When the prospect agrees, follow the agency's proposal and payment process. Confirm the selected scope and pass accurate requirements to the account or delivery team. Do not promise a starting date, extra revision or special result that has not been approved. A good handover helps the client receive what was sold and allows the delivery team to begin with a clear brief.

## Working from home: applicant preparation checklist

Remote applicants can apply from anywhere in Sri Lanka, but working from home still requires professional calls and dependable communication. Before choosing this option, consider whether your usual workspace is quiet enough to hear customers clearly and whether you can take agreed calls without interruptions. Test your phone audio and internet connection in that space rather than relying on how they perform elsewhere.

Have a practical way to record notes while speaking. Depending on the agreed tools, this may involve a smartphone, a laptop or a shared reporting sheet. Ask the team which tools are required and whether any equipment is supplied. A remote option does not automatically mean that hardware, internet, a SIM or a call allowance is included.

Protect customer information as carefully at home as you would in an office. Use lead details only for the assigned agency work, avoid forwarding them to personal groups and do not copy them into an unrelated contact list. Keep screens and conversations away from people who do not need access. Follow the team's confidentiality agreement and instructions for storing or deleting records.

If you are studying, caring for family or working another job, describe your realistic availability when applying. Agree calling blocks that you can consistently attend, including time for preparation and reporting. Flexible work does not mean calling prospects at any hour or changing commitments without telling the coordinator.

## How to handle common sales questions responsibly

Applicants do not need a perfect answer to every objection. A useful response acknowledges the concern, checks what the prospect means and gives only information you can support. These examples are learning guidance, not customer conversations that actually occurred.

- **“Your package is too expensive.”** Ask which scope or budget the prospect is comparing. Explain the approved deliverables without criticising competitors or inventing a discount. Refer any alternative scope to the team.
- **“Can you guarantee sales?”** Explain that a marketing service has an agreed scope and measurement process, but customer demand, the offer and follow-up affect outcomes. Do not turn an aspiration into a guarantee.
- **“I need to think about it.”** Ask whether any information would help their decision and agree whether they want a follow-up. Give them space to review the proposal.
- **“Can you start tomorrow?”** Check availability with the delivery team. Do not commit to a deadline before the team has confirmed the brief, payment requirements and resources.
- **“Another agency includes more.”** Compare written scopes rather than assumptions. Differences may concern quantities, platforms, approvals, advertising spend or reporting. Keep the conversation factual and respectful.

Knowing when to ask for help is part of the role. Escalate technical, payment and delivery questions rather than improvising an answer that creates a problem for the client later.
