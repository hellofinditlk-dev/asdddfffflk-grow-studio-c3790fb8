## The problem most small businesses have with social media

If any of these sound familiar, you're not alone:

- **You post when you remember.** A busy week means nothing goes out, and the page looks inactive.
- **You boost posts but get only likes.** Money goes out, but messages and sales don't come in.
- **Your posts don't look professional.** Phone photos and quick Canva designs make a good business look small.
- **You don't have time to reply.** Messages sit for hours, and customers buy elsewhere.
- **You don't know what's working.** You can't tell which post or ad brought a customer.
- **Your nephew or a freelancer used to do it,** and then stopped.

Social media works for small businesses when it's consistent, looks professional, and points people to one clear next step: a WhatsApp message, a call or an order. That's what we set up for you.

## What small businesses actually need from social media

You don't need to be everywhere or post three times a day. Most small businesses in Sri Lanka need five things:

**1. One or two platforms done well.** For most small businesses that means Facebook and Instagram, with TikTok if your customers are young. LinkedIn only matters if you sell to other businesses. Read [which social media platform is best for your business](/blog/best-social-media-platform-sri-lanka-business).

**2. Regular, good-looking posts.** Three or four quality posts a week beat daily posts that look rushed.

**3. Short videos.** Reels are one format to test alongside images; compare enquiries and sales rather than assuming higher reach. You don't need a film crew: a well-planned 30-second reel at your shop is enough.

**4. A small, well-targeted ad budget.** Even LKR 40,000 to 60,000 a month in ads, aimed at the right people in your area, can bring a steady flow of messages.

**5. Fast replies.** A customer who messages and hears back in minutes is far more likely to buy.

## Common small business social media mistakes (and how we fix them)

1. **Boosting every post.** Only posts with a clear offer should get budget. We choose what to promote. See our [social media boosting service](/social-media-boosting-service-sri-lanka).
2. **No WhatsApp button.** If customers can't message you in one tap, many won't. We set it up on every page and ad.
3. **No prices.** "Inbox for price" loses customers who just want a rough idea. We help you show starting prices where it makes sense.
4. **Only selling, never helping.** Posts that teach, show behind the scenes or share customer stories build trust that makes selling easier.
5. **Ignoring video.** Test reels alongside images to see what your customers respond to. Every plan includes reels.
6. **Inconsistent posting.** A content calendar fixes this. We plan a month ahead.

## Signs you're ready to hand over your social media

You don't need an agency on day one. But it's usually time when:

- You've gone two weeks or more without posting, more than once.
- You're spending money on boosts but can't say what you got back.

- Your competitors' pages look more professional than yours, even though your product is better.
- You've tried a freelancer or a family member, and it stopped.
- Your business is growing and your time is worth more spent on sales, staff or suppliers.

If two or more of these are true, compare a monthly plan against your available time, contribution margin and measured enquiries before deciding.

## What results can a small business realistically expect?

We'd rather set honest expectations than overpromise.

**In the first month,** focus on publishing the approved content, checking tracking and establishing your starting metrics. If you're running ads, measure whether enquiries arrive and review the campaign if they do not; increased reach and engagement are not guaranteed.

**By the second and third month,** review which content and audiences produced useful enquiries, bookings or sales. Use the recorded evidence to adjust the plan rather than expecting a fixed timetable for results.

**Over six months,** a consistently active page builds trust. Customers recognise your brand, come back, and recommend you. Track whether your cost per qualified enquiry improves; lower costs are not guaranteed.

What affects your results: how competitive your category is, your prices and offers, your ad budget, and how quickly you reply to messages. We'll talk through all of these on your free strategy call.

### Is social media marketing worth it for a small business?
It can be worthwhile if your customers use the selected platforms and the enquiries produce enough contribution margin to justify the cost. ### Which platform should a small business start with?
For most small businesses, Facebook and Instagram, because that's where most Sri Lankan customers are and they can be managed together. Add TikTok if your customers are younger, and LinkedIn only if you sell to other businesses.

Twelve to fifteen good posts a month, plus a few reels, is enough for most small businesses. Quality and consistency matter more than volume.
